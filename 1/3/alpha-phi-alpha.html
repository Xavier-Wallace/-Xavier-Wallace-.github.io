Founded

December 4, 1906

Founding Location

Cornell University, Ithaca, New York

History

Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Inc. was founded by seven college men, known as the Jewels, at Cornell University. The organization became the first intercollegiate historically Black fraternity and was established to provide support, leadership, and fellowship for African American students.

During a time when racial discrimination limited opportunities for Black students, Alpha Phi Alpha created a network of brotherhood and academic excellence. The fraternity quickly expanded to colleges and universities throughout the United States.

Since its founding, Alpha Phi Alpha has promoted scholarship, leadership development, and community service. The organization has consistently encouraged members to become leaders in their careers and communities.

Impact and Legacy

Alpha Phi Alpha has played a major role in African American history. One of its most famous members was Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., who helped lead the Civil Rights Movement.

The fraternity has sponsored mentoring programs, voter education initiatives, scholarships, and community outreach projects for more than a century.

Today, Alpha Phi Alpha continues to uphold its mission of developing leaders, promoting academic achievement, and serving communities around the world.
